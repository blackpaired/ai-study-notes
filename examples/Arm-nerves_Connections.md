## 0. The core rules {.gray}

**Rule 1 – One compartment = one nerve.**
Muscles in the same fascial compartment usually do the same job and are supplied by the same nerve.
→ Anterior arm = musculocutaneous nerve, posterior arm = radial nerve.

**Rule 2 – Dorsal and ventral layer.**
In the embryo, every limb bud has a **dorsal muscle layer (extensors)** and a **ventral muscle layer (flexors)**, each with its own nerves:

| | Dorsal layer (extensors) | Ventral layer (flexors) |
|---|---|---|
| **Arm** | axillary n., radial n. | musculocutaneous n., median n., ulnar n. |

**Rule 3 – Innervation shows origin, not function.**
If a muscle's nerve doesn't fit its function, the muscle comes from the other layer.

**Remember:** "Tell me your nerve, and I'll tell you where you come from."

## 1. Musculocutaneous nerve {.blue}

| Muscle | Function |
|---|---|
| Biceps brachii | Flexion at the elbow, supination, anteversion |
| Brachialis (lateral part inconstantly also radial n.) | Flexion at the elbow |
| Coracobrachialis | Anteversion |

**Why?** This is rule 1 in its purest form: the **anterior compartment of the arm** belongs to the musculocutaneous nerve. It pierces the coracobrachialis, runs between biceps and brachialis and supplies all three. Then it becomes a skin nerve of the forearm – hence "musculo-**cutaneous**": first muscle, then skin.

**Why can the brachialis get the radial nerve too?** Its lateral part borders the **extensor compartment**, where the radial nerve runs. A muscle at the border of two compartments can be supplied by both.

**Mnemonic:** "**BBC**": **B**iceps, **B**rachialis, **C**oracobrachialis = musculocutaneous nerve.

## 2. Radial nerve

| Muscle | Function |
|---|---|
| Triceps brachii (all heads) | Extension at the elbow |
| Brachioradialis | Flexion at the elbow (!) |
| Extensors of the forearm | Extension of wrist and fingers |

**Why?** The radial nerve is **the** nerve of the dorsal layer of the whole arm: everything at the back that extends.

**Why the brachioradialis, although it flexes?** It belongs to the **dorsal layer** by development and only became a flexor because it lies in front of the elbow axis. The innervation shows its origin (rule 3), not its function.

**Mnemonic:** "**Radial = rear**": everything at the back of the arm that extends belongs to the radial nerve.

## 3. Overview {.purple}

| Function | Muscle group | Nerve | Layer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elbow flexors | Biceps, brachialis | Musculocutaneous n. | ventral |
| Elbow extensors | Triceps | Radial n. | dorsal |
| Exception | Brachioradialis (flexes) | Radial n. | dorsal |

> **If you take only one thing away:** the **compartment** decides the nerve. Where the innervation breaks the pattern, the muscle comes from another layer.
